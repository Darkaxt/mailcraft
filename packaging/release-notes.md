**MailCraft 0.12.27** — uses far less memory
- MailCraft no longer keeps the text of your newest mail in memory when it starts. The list takes its previews from the search index, and a message reads its text when you open it.
- With a large mailbox this saves a few hundred MB of memory. Nothing looks or works differently.
- Everything from 0.12.26 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.26 in short** — choose how messages show in the list
- A new button beside the sort order picks the message rows: Compact (one line), Normal (subject first) or Extended (sender first, as before).
- Normal shows the folder and subject with the time on top, and the sender and a line of the message under them. More mail fits on screen.
- Everything from 0.12.25 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.25 in short** — the folder leads the subject; previews without setup
- In the unified mailbox and in searches of every mailbox, a message's folder now starts its subject line: its emoji tag (or folder icon), its name, then the subject. Point at it to see whose account it is.
- Clicking an attachment, in a message or in a draft, previews it right away. You no longer need to turn on the Preview Pane or attachment previews first.
- After you sign in again, the account warning clears right away instead of a minute later.
- Everything from 0.12.24 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.24 in short** — one Folders for all accounts, clearer stars
- The star after a folder's name means it is in Favorites. Click the star to remove it.
- "Folders" in the unified mailbox is now its own entry for all accounts, not your Outlook Inbox. It has no star, and right-click offers New Folder.
- The Tag menu no longer suggests a star, which looked like the Favorites star.
- Everything from 0.12.23 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.23 in short** — the emoji browser stays open
- Clicking a group or the search box in a folder's Tag menu no longer closes it. Only picking an emoji does.
- All emoji now scroll in one list with a heading per group. The group buttons jump to it, and the emoji under the pointer shows its name.
- Everything from 0.12.22 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.22 in short** — every emoji for folder tags
- A folder's Tag menu now has every emoji your system can show, in groups (smileys, people, animals, food, travel, activities, objects, symbols, flags), with a search by name.
- Everything from 0.12.21 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.21 in short** — favourites show their star in the unified mailbox
- A folder you added to Favorites now has its small star in the unified mailbox's folder list too, not only in its account's list.
- Everything from 0.12.20 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.20 in short** — MailCraft opens your mailbox however you start it
- The Start Menu, a mailto: link or a message file you open now bring up your own mailbox, with the same setup as your usual shortcut. If MailCraft is already open, they go to that window.
- Its temporary files go to the same folder as with your usual shortcut.
- Everything from 0.12.19 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.19 in short** — sorting Favorites works
- Dragging a favourite up or down Favorites now moves it. Before, the line showed but the folder stayed where it was.
- Everything from 0.12.18 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.18 in short** — updates when you choose
- When a new version is out, the update button at the bottom right shows a blue dot. Click it to see what's new.
- Update now downloads it, closes MailCraft, installs it and opens it again with your mailbox. Windows asks for your OK first.
- Nothing is downloaded or installed by itself any more, and nothing is installed when you close MailCraft.
- Everything from 0.12.17 stays.

**MailCraft 0.12.15 in short** — the latest MailCraft improvements, merged in
- Brings in 484 changes from the main MailCraft project. Among them: one message that fails to download no longer stops Send/Receive; File › Info says which messages were left out, with Try again.
- Large mailboxes save with Save As without running out of memory, and Save Message As writes the message exactly as it was received.
- Microsoft accounts ask for their permissions on one sign-in page.
- Pasted tables survive Find and Replace.
- Everything from 0.12.14 stays (folder changes that reach the server, the attachment preview pane, recent folders).

**MailCraft 0.12.14 in short** — folders that reach the server, previews beside your mail
- Renaming, moving or deleting a folder of a Gmail or other IMAP account now changes it on the server too. A deleted folder's mail goes to Deleted Items first, and the folder goes in the next Send/Receive, unless new mail arrived in it. The Inbox and the server's own folders are never changed.
- A folder moved into Gmail's Trash is deleted instead, so no label is left under Trash.
- A preview pane on the right (View › Preview Pane, Alt+P) shows the attachment you click: text, tables, pictures, PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Photoshop and SVG files, web pages, saved messages and the list of a ZIP. Drag its edge to make it wider; MailCraft remembers.
- Attachments of a draft can be previewed too: click one in the message you are writing.
- The question before sending lists each attached file with its size.
- Recent folders: the folders you opened or filed into last are listed under Favorites, and Move to Folder and Copy to Folder offer your Favorites and recent folders first.
- Everything from 0.12.13 stays (a quieter status bar, a tidier folder tree).

- A mailbox's bodies and attachments take much less space on disk: content that shrinks (text and HTML mail, most PDFs) is kept compressed in its folder beside the mailbox file, and what was written before is compressed in the background a little at a time; every file is still checked against its SHA-256 when it is read, and the mailbox file itself doesn't change.

- Search finds a word inside a longer one (`voice` finds `invoice`), plain and case-insensitive by default, with Match case, Match whole word and Regular expression toggles in the search box and a Search in menu (subject, people, body, attachments). Each result has a score from 0 to 100 (a whole-word match is 100) and the list can be sorted By Relevance.
- The content index is smaller and is written in batches; it indexes the Inbox and newest mail first, skips Junk E-mail and Deleted Items (searched directly instead), retries failed documents with a back-off, refuses oversized archives, and stops at once when the window closes. An index from an older version is rebuilt on first start.

- Import a .pst File now brings in notes and journal entries too, and mail kept as rich text keeps its HTML. The import is no longer a preview. Notes and the journal have no window of their own yet ([#815](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/815)).
- OpenPGP Digital signatures are now checked in the reading pane; Public keys can be imported, listed and removed ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- File › Options › Security lists your own certificates and keys and imports a `.p12` or an OpenPGP secret key; the Public keys kept for other people are listed and removed there, an OpenPGP key is marked as verified after comparing its fingerprint, and a contact's card shows its certificates ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).

**Public beta.** For Microsoft accounts (personal, or work or school) MailCraft is a working mail, calendar and contacts program, kept in step with Outlook on the web; keep Outlook to hand while you try it, and tell us in an issue if anything is lost or out of step. Much of what a daily mail program needs is still to come, and other kinds of account are welcome but not part of the beta. What works is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today).

MailCraft keeps your mailbox in its settings folder and saves as you go; one window per mailbox. With a Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com account you sign in on Microsoft's page, and the sign-in is kept by the system's credential store (Windows, macOS, and the Secret Service of GNOME Keyring or KWallet on Linux). What works, and how far each part is from Outlook, is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today) and in `docs/gaps.md`.

The builds are not signed with a publisher certificate yet: Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will ask before running them.
