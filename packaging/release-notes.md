**MailCraft 0.12.1 in short** — upstream MailCraft 0.12.0 merged into the Windows fork
- S/MIME and OpenPGP signatures are checked in the reading pane; your certificates and keys, and other people's Public keys, are managed in File › Options › Security.
- Import a .pst File brings in calendar items, tasks, notes, journal entries and large distribution lists.
- Gmail's categories (Primary, Social, Promotions, Updates, Forums) as folders or tabs.
- Optional attachment previews (off by default) with local PdfCraft or PhotoCraft; an attachment Microsoft Defender flagged is never previewed.
- Empty Folder on a huge folder takes about a second; Delete, Archive and Junk of many messages scale with their number.
- Everything from 0.11.6 stays: Defender scans of what you read, agents' questions that wait for you, the unified mailbox and its rules.

**What's new**
- Enable attachment previews in File › Options to preview PDFs and supported images in the reading pane with local PdfCraft or PhotoCraft. [#1053](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/1053)
- Attachments of a message you read are scanned by Microsoft Defender from a temporary copy, never in the mailbox; one it flags is marked in the message and asks before it is opened or saved, and nothing is deleted.
- Headless, an agent's question waits for you for up to a day instead of two minutes; the agent is told at once and reads the outcome with `agent.answer`.
- Headless MailCraft asks the owner about what an agent wants (sending, permanent deletion and the like) in a small always-on-top window instead of refusing; the answer is no unless the owner clicks yes.
- A mailbox's bodies and attachments take much less space on disk: content that shrinks (text and HTML mail, most PDFs) is kept compressed in its folder beside the mailbox file, and what was written before is compressed in the background a little at a time; every file is still checked against its SHA-256 when it is read, and the mailbox file itself doesn't change.
- **Unified mailbox.** A root at the top of the folder pane holds every Inbox together, the filing account's real folder tree (its Inbox shown as Unfiled) and the saved search folders; the + beside it makes a new search folder, and its menu picks the filing account and how origin colours are drawn.
- Account and alias colours are strong, saturated colours, shown as a slim stripe or across the whole row; the choice sits beside the sort menu and is remembered.
- Search finds a word inside a longer one (`voice` finds `invoice`), plain and case-insensitive by default, with Match case, Match whole word and Regular expression toggles in the search box and a Search in menu (subject, people, body, attachments). Each result has a score from 0 to 100 (a whole-word match is 100) and the list can be sorted By Relevance.
- The content index is smaller and is written in batches; it indexes the Inbox and newest mail first, skips Junk E-mail and Deleted Items (searched directly instead), retries failed documents with a back-off, refuses oversized archives, and stops at once when the window closes. An index from an older version is rebuilt on first start.
- Pictures are downloaded by default for mail outside Junk E-mail. Tracking pictures (known trackers, one-pixel, hidden or off-page images) are blocked; a shield above the message says how many and can load them anyway.
- The reader's controls float over the message: the shield, a rendered/source toggle and Message appearance. The message source is one continuous, coloured view of up to 16 MiB.
- Import a .pst File now brings in notes and journal entries too, and mail kept as rich text keeps its HTML. The import is no longer a preview. Notes and the journal have no window of their own yet ([#815](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/815)).
- OpenPGP Digital signatures are now checked in the reading pane; Public keys can be imported, listed and removed ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- File › Options › Security lists your own certificates and keys and imports a `.p12` or an OpenPGP secret key; the Public keys kept for other people are listed and removed there, an OpenPGP key is marked as verified after comparing its fingerprint, and a contact's card shows its certificates ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- Help › Report a Problem can now send a report straight to the MailCraft team, no GitHub account needed: press Send report, and the window shows the report's reference. Only what the window shows goes, and only when you press Send; the report is kept private unless the team makes a real problem public on GitHub, where you can choose to be mentioned by your GitHub username. Open on GitHub is still there ([#956](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/956)).
- Import a .pst File now brings in calendar items, with their repeat patterns and time zones, and tasks. Nothing is sent to anybody ([#815](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/815)).
- Typing `* ` or `- ` at the start of a line in a message now starts a bulleted list, and `1. ` or `1) ` a numbered one. Undo straight afterwards puts back what you typed, and File › Options › Mail › Automatic bulleted and numbered lists turns it off ([#973](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/973)).
- **Pictures in all mail from the message.** The bar above a message whose pictures were not downloaded now also offers **Download pictures in all mail**, the switch from File › Options in one click. It first says what that means: every sender's server learns when you opened a message and from which internet address. It stays off until you say yes, mail in Junk E-mail still never downloads pictures by itself, and File › Options turns it off again. ([#910](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/910))
- Each account can have a colour (Account Settings › Colour; a new account is given one): a dot beside its name in the folder pane, and a stripe on its mail in a search of All Mailboxes, so that whose mail is whose is seen at a glance ([#893](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/893)).
- Import a .pst File brings in the members of a large distribution list too: past about 15,000 bytes Outlook keeps them in a form of their own, which the import now reads, up to 1,000 members a list ([#843](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/843)).
- **Pictures in replies and forwards.** When you reply to or forward a message whose pictures are written into it rather than attached, the pictures now stay in the original below your text and go with your message, as they do from Outlook. Only pictures MailCraft would show are carried, within the same limits on size and number; nothing is downloaded for them ([#602](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/602)).
- The fork's current release and application checks target Windows only; macOS, Linux, FreeBSD and web delivery are paused with their source recipes preserved.
- A contact's S/MIME certificates and OpenPGP keys in a vCard (`KEY`) are kept when the card is imported and written back when contacts are exported ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- An S/MIME Digitally signed message says what checking its signature found, in a bar above it: verified, signed but the signer not verified, changed after it was signed, or can't be checked (an algorithm that's no longer safe, for one), with Details of who signed and their certificate; its row in the list wears a seal of the same. Trust comes from the certification authorities your system trusts for mail (on Linux its mail bundle, on Windows the roots trusted for secure mail; none on FreeBSD, whose authorities are for web sites, and none yet on macOS). An opaque signed message shows what it holds, its attachments too; revocation isn't checked yet ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- Switch a message between Light, Dark and the app theme directly in the reader. Preview the complete current unsent draft in an independent light or dark presentation, then return to editing without saving, sending or changing its HTML.
- All inboxes combines the real inboxes across accounts without copying mail. Filtering, search and replies retain each message's account. Account and alias context menus offer persistent pastel or custom origin colours and reset; an alias can override its account's colour. Combined rows also name their origin so colour is not the only cue.
- File received mail from every account through Outlook's real folder tree. Drops learn exact sender rules automatically; competing destinations open a focused review with reuse, sender-only reroute/history, and durable one-time choices. Account/alias origin colours and reply identities survive relocation. Provider placement and unavailable messages remain explicit until confirmed.
- Saved search folders keep named local searches in the folder pane, with exact phrase, case-sensitive and alias filters and an explicit folder, mailbox or combined-inbox scope. Create one from the current or advanced search, preview it, edit it, or remove its definition with Undo. Definitions travel with the mailbox and are available through native commands. A missing folder binding is shown as unavailable for repair; saving or deleting a search never relocates messages or creates a provider folder. Search results retain index coverage reporting.
- Windows x64 packages include the source-bound native attachment indexing workers, local OCR, English/Spanish/Greek models and their licences. Portable and MSI formats carry the same manifest-bound runtime. This fork checks its own release repository and uses its own checksum-signing keys, preserving update preferences.
- Windows window controls share MailCraft's top row with search, quick actions and the account controls. Narrow windows retain search through compact diagnostic buttons. Native dragging, resize, system menu and Snap Layouts use the same rendered caption geometry; closing retains MailCraft's unsaved-draft handling.
- Read HTML mail in a coherent dark palette with separate background, text and border adaptation inspired by Dark Reader. Preserve readable sender-designed dark colours and original image pixels. Body text reaches 4.5:1 contrast; high contrast uses the owner's strong palette. Accent/high-contrast changes refresh already-open mail without changing its source or reply content.
- Search retained mail and supported attachment contents offline with literal phrases, case-sensitive matching, explicit coverage and located native/OCR evidence.
- A Gmail account's Inbox shows Gmail's categories, Primary, Social, Promotions, Updates and Forums, as five folders under it, each with its unread count, whether the account signs in with Google or an app password. Account Settings can hide them, or keep desktop alerts to mail in Primary, and the search finds them with `gmail:social` and the rest ([#911](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/911)).
- View › Gmail Categories can show them as a row of tabs above the Inbox instead, each with its unread count ([#911](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/911)).
- Native reading-pane Source/Message switching, selectable formatted HTML and plain text, and complete reconstructed `.eml` export with verified streaming attachments. MCP source/export pages support bounded, explicit continuation and owned cleanup.
- Inspect a message's cross-account filing state through `mail.filingStatus`, including pending placement and source removal, without fetching its body or changing mail.
- Inspect SimpleLogin's reported original sender and receiving alias while preserving private reply routing. Account-scoped alias filters work across downloaded folders, with explicit metadata gaps, separate search/rule fields and read-only MCP inspection.
- Manage local rules from their destination folders, with regex, case controls, exceptions, copy actions and non-mutating previews. Missing destinations pause a local rule before acting; action failures remain visible. Server-owned originals and cross-account placement safety are preserved.
- Compatible sender filing rules consolidate automatically into an editable sender list, retaining their originals and existing-mail execution scope. Nonadjacent groups combine only when intervening account or exact-sender constraints prove independence. Ordinary filing rules need no MCP owner prompt; deleting rule effects remain protected.
- Check overlapping rules across destination folders and prevent competing moves before any rule actions. Classification follows newly detected received messages, including historical/custom-folder sync, with durable content waiting and read-only MCP diagnostics.
- Deleting a destination folder or moving it under Deleted Items removes its move/copy rules, including descendant destinations and cross-account sources. Reversible folder changes restore their rules on undo; provider deletions remain pending until acknowledged, with refusals retained explicitly.
- Folder discovery retains exact provider labels separately from normalized display and sync names for reliable destination identity.
- Inspect resident mail and index buffers with opt-in memory attribution; ordinary mailbox inspection remains unchanged and the diagnostic does not load content.
- Run the live mail owner with `--headless` for authenticated MCP access and synchronization without creating a window or graphics runtime. Existing credentials and save/close protection are retained; interactive consent requires the desktop.
- Background desktop use supports native hidden startup, observed visibility/focus through MCP, and explicit hide and idle-only normal-close tools that preserve open work and wait for acknowledgement and saving.

**Fixes**
- Headless MailCraft can close normally after an agent opened and sent or closed a draft.
- The reading pane also recognises the open-tracking pictures of Salesforce Audience Studio (Krux), Alibaba (AliExpress) analytics and MoEngage, and blocks them with the other trackers.
- Week numbers in the calendar line up: in the Month view and in the date navigator every digit has a place of one width, where a number such as 20 used to start left of the others ([#1009](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1009)).
- HTML newsletters keep headings up to 48 pixels, inline line height, image spacing, and the width of centered tables that size to their contents. CSS table rows and cells use the same layout as HTML table tags ([#1021](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1021)).
- The Flatpak uses installed host fonts for Japanese and other scripts, and links in the reading pane are underlined close to their text ([#1016](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/1016)).
- A message an earlier version left in the Outbox from a draft begun on another device, and that a run then held, goes again with Send Again (Microsoft and Exchange server accounts; [#738](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/738)).
- Fork QA (0.11.3): SimpleLogin alias folders, colours and original senders are given only to mail relayed by SimpleLogin to that alias, so copied headers can't pose as an alias; one unreadable alias colour no longer drops an account's others. Rules: deleting and filing into Deleted Items count as one competing destination in conflict review, manual runs and previews classify received mail only, filing into a shared mailbox asks the owner as moving mail there does, and editing a merged rule's senders drops its stale merge history. Offline search: an attachment read before its message was downloaded is read again after, a document whose extraction crashes is recorded as failed instead of stopping indexing, a hung document or OCR worker is ended after two minutes, and closed source exports free their slots. Closing waits for open search inspections and gives up on an unreleased MCP bridge after 30 seconds; a failed headless save is retried and never makes later closes impossible.
- Import a .pst File no longer brings mail kept only as rich text in with an empty body: its text is the body, and the rich text is kept as an attachment ([#815](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/815)).
- An IMAP account with more than 2,000 folders has them all read now, up to 10,000 in one Send/Receive, each in its place in the folder tree, and the folder pane shows every folder of an account (it showed at most 4,000); with more than that, the message says how many were left out and the first of them ([#991](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/991)).
- In English, dates now follow your system's regional format: in the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, India, South Africa and elsewhere outside the US, Canada and the Philippines they are written day first (`14/10/2026`, `14 Oct 2026`), and the clock and the first day of the week are the region's unless you chose your own in File › Options. On Linux and FreeBSD the format is read from `LC_TIME` ([#978](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/978)).
- An Exchange server account no longer downloads every message in full: like a Microsoft account, it keeps mail in full only within its Mail to keep offline (12 months unless you change it in File › Info or Account Settings), and older mail as headers and a preview that download when you open them. "Messages to download per folder" in File › Options is for IMAP accounts ([#966](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/966)).
- In a plain-text message, Font, Font Size, Font Color and Text Highlight Color are greyed with the other formatting buttons, and say why ([#862](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/862)).
- The reasons why your automatic replies weren't changed, and why desktop alerts can't be shown, are now translated on their own, apart from the heading they follow, so a translator writes each heading once ([#912](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/912)).
- The Reminders window answers Dismiss, Dismiss All, Snooze and its close button while the main window is minimized ([#771](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/771)).
- Sign-in and server errors of IMAP and SMTP accounts are shown in the interface's language in Send/Receive problems, the status bar and Add Account, not only in English; what a server says for itself is still shown as the server wrote it ([#872](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/872)).
- Preserve the source-pinned native runtime asset when refreshing the prepared release draft, keeping its numeric download identity and verified checksum intact.
- Complete message exports preserve a retained signed or encrypted MIME body while streaming verified content, report its provenance, and refuse missing or damaged originals instead of silently reconstructing them.
- Help › Report a Problem no longer offers Send by Email: reports go through GitHub, so they arrive in one place where they can be triaged. A way to send a report without a GitHub account is coming ([#956](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/956)).
- Text in Calibri, Cambria and other fonts that carry small bitmaps beside their outlines is drawn whole again: since 0.11.0 most of its letters were missing, drawn in white ([#889](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/889)).
- A digitally signed (S/MIME or OpenPGP) message moved or copied to another IMAP account, saved as an `.eml` file or forwarded as an attachment now carries its signed body exactly as it arrived, so its signature still checks there; signed and encrypted mail is kept as it came for checking in a later version ([#30](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/30)).
- Windows 32-bit builds retain the locked process dependency with its packed handle copied before borrowing; spawn ownership and consent behavior are preserved.
- Windows x64 release inputs use an exact committed asset ID and checksum, fetched before compilation through GitHub's read-only asset API. Source-hash inputs keep identical LF bytes on Windows and Unix checkouts.
- Preserve lazy headless mail checks and manual-only Microsoft To Do setup when integrating upstream improvements. Deferred rule content fetches respect queued mail and locally edited drafts.
- Local checksum signing uses the existing Rust signer on Git for Windows, where native OpenSSL cannot read Bash process-substitution descriptors. Private keys stay in memory.
- Preserve explicitly changed preferences, including offline mode, across normal headless restarts without replacing desktop layout or unrelated settings.
- Wait for startup content validation before retiring older cached mail, avoiding false missing-content warnings. Report content availability and refuse to present a cached preview as a full MCP message read. Request missing source content through the connected app without opening a window or replacing another foreground download.
- Native sign-in keeps its local callback listener ready while you finish on the provider's page, without a five-minute app deadline. Cancel Sign-in still ends the request explicitly.
- Remove blanket consent prompts for reads and reversible edits, including ordinary cross-account filing and saving a composer to Drafts. Protect unsaved signature edits and reject destructive approvals after their target state changes.
- Headless rules checks no longer open an invisible permission dialog that blocks normal close. MCP can explicitly request Microsoft's approval page; the person still approves there, and background checks never open it automatically.
- Keep large saved message bodies on disk during headless startup instead of warming the desktop reading cache. Content validation and on-demand offline reads are preserved.
- Microsoft To Do is off until explicitly connected from File › Info. Checking mail no longer starts its permission prompt; disconnecting keeps existing tasks and mail sign-ins.
- Connecting or signing back into another account queues its first download when Send/Receive is already running, without cancelling the current account or skipping the new one. The account-setup page says when that download is waiting.
- Coordinated live-app MCP workflows can use `--no-replay` to retain an unknown request outcome instead of reconnecting and sending it again.
- Gmail sign-in uses the fork's dedicated Google Desktop registration and native provider consent. Source builds can configure their own matching client-ID/secret pair as described in docs/google-accounts.md.
- The MCP server reads each request within a bound: an over-long one is answered with an error and the server carries on with the next (AU3-R4).
- With MCP connected to the running window, the bridge reads the window's answers within a bound, and an over-long answer from the control port is refused rather than read to the end (AU3-R3).
- With MCP connected to the running window, a command whose answer was lost is no longer sent a second time: the agent is told the app may have run it (AU3-R2).
- An agent connected through MCP now sees a message's source, the searches made from a message's subject or sender, an online meeting's link, and a contact's details and categories marked as text written by somebody else, like the rest of a message (AU3-R1) ([GHSA-hqm2-x252-pj57](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/security/advisories/GHSA-hqm2-x252-pj57)).
- File › Open & Export has Save Message As, and a message saved under a name typed without an extension is saved as `.eml`.
- A folder deleted for good in a Microsoft or Exchange server account is deleted on the server only when the server holds nothing more in it than you had: when mail arrived there since, or a folder was made in it elsewhere, it isn't deleted, comes back with what is new, and MailCraft says how many new items it has; when what the server holds there can't be checked, nothing is deleted (AU3-H7).
- On Windows, how much of a mailbox is read into memory now follows the computer's real memory instead of a fixed 8 GiB.
- Opening another mailbox while mail is being sent or received no longer lets that work change the newly opened mailbox, and a message whose sending was cut short (another mailbox opened, or MailCraft stopped) is held in the Outbox as one that may have been sent rather than sent a second time (AU3-08, AU3-09, AU3-10, AU3-21, AU3-26, AU3-V8).
- A draft begun on another device and sent from MailCraft is sent with the next Send/Receive instead of waiting in the Outbox, including one an earlier version left there (Microsoft and Exchange server accounts).
- Mail is vouched for only by its own server's word, also on Microsoft 365 and for mail moved onto a server; Safe Senders respect a failed check; server-kept meetings are changed only by their organizer; link warnings cover every layout of a message; a file is listed unless it is a picture the body shows (AU3-11, AU3-12, AU3-13, AU3-14, AU3-15, AU3-V5).
- A remembered password or sign-in is used only for the account it was remembered for, as that account is, a certificate it trusts included; a mailbox where two accounts have the same address asks you to confirm its servers before any is used; Remove all data removes every remembered sign-in it read, and removing an account removes its sign-in under every name it was kept by; Exchange server setup asks before your password goes to a server in your domain other than its Autodiscover server; a change to a meeting in a shared mailbox waits until sending from it is allowed (AU3-02, AU3-03, AU3-18, AU3-19, AU3-V1, AU3-V2).
- A late download of a message no longer changes a draft edited or queued to send meanwhile, and a change waiting through a server renumbering goes to the one matching message or is dropped with a word (AU3-16, AU3-17).
- Nine more kinds of file that run or hand off to a program that runs what is in them are never opened from mail, and a file kind with a non-ASCII letter is asked about before it opens instead of being taken for a harmless one (AU3-V3, AU3-V4).
- Security fixes AU3-24, AU3-25, AU3-23 and AU3-20: a yes to an agent's question now covers only what the question showed; anything that changed while it was asked is refused, and Empty Folder leaves mail and folders that arrived in the meantime. AU3-V6: on Windows and macOS, every file saved from mail, an agent's saves included, is marked as coming from the Internet when the system lets the mark be written; Linux and FreeBSD have no such mark ([GHSA-ppcq-57r6-r2cm](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/security/advisories/GHSA-ppcq-57r6-r2cm)).
- A mailbox file that would take more memory to open than MailCraft allows for it on this computer is refused with an error when it is opened, in every form it comes in, instead of using up the memory (AU3-05, AU3-06, AU3-07).
- Reading HTML mail and reopening a draft stay within bounded memory and time (AU3-01, AU3-22) ([GHSA-qwvc-cmh6-9h8x](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/security/advisories/GHSA-qwvc-cmh6-9h8x)).
- Safer releases: the signing steps are kept apart from the build (AU3-04).
- A folder that a Microsoft 365 or Exchange server keeps for itself, such as RSS Feeds, can no longer end up in Deleted Items here while the server still has it where it was: once the server says it is one of its own, the folder goes back where the server has it, you are told why, and Delete Folder, Rename and Move are greyed out for it from then on (and refused with the reason when an agent or a script asks). A move or deletion the server turns down for another reason puts the folder back too, rather than stopping Send/Receive on it every time ([#1069](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1069)).
- An imported `.ics` file no longer hides in your Microsoft 365 calendar without reaching it: when the account's calendar was the only one, an imported appointment went into it as if it were there, was never sent, and nothing offered to send it. It now goes into the calendar **On this computer**, with the bar above the calendar and **Move to Calendar** in its menu offering to move it to your account's calendar; one that an earlier import left behind goes there at the next Send/Receive ([#1050](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1050)).
- Empty Folder on a folder of many thousands of messages no longer leaves the window not responding: a folder of 200,000 now empties in about a second (it took about an hour), and Delete, Delete Permanently, Archive and Junk of a large selection are as quick. Undo works as it did, and the server is told the same as before ([#1037](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/1037)).
- A server that can't be reached because it shares no encryption with MailCraft (often an old Exchange 2010 offering only CBC cipher suites) now says so, and that its administrator can turn on the AES-GCM suites, instead of a general connection failure ([#977](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/977)).
- Add Account recognises legacy Outlook.com custom domains from their Hotmail MX records and selects Microsoft sign-in instead of guessed IMAP and SMTP settings; owners can keep their custom-domain address after confirming that Microsoft's returned address is an alias of the same mailbox ([#1070](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/pull/1070)).
- Connecting Outlook's Favorites for an account whose organisation requires administrator approval for reading mailbox settings now shows the administrator approval message once instead of repeatedly prompting to connect ([#986](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/986)).

**Public beta.** For Microsoft accounts (personal, or work or school) MailCraft is a working mail, calendar and contacts program, kept in step with Outlook on the web; keep Outlook to hand while you try it, and tell us in an issue if anything is lost or out of step. Much of what a daily mail program needs is still to come, and other kinds of account are welcome but not part of the beta. What works is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today).

MailCraft keeps your mailbox in its settings folder and saves as you go; one window per mailbox. With a Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com account you sign in on Microsoft's page, and the sign-in is kept by the system's credential store (Windows, macOS, and the Secret Service of GNOME Keyring or KWallet on Linux). What works, and how far each part is from Outlook, is in the [README](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY#what-works-today) and in `docs/gaps.md`.

The builds are not signed with a publisher certificate yet: Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper will ask before running them.
